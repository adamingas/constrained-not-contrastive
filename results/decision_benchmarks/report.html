Constrained Qwen versus CLM

Public tool-selection and Wikipedia-navigation experiments, with frozen prompts, cached decisions, and no planner-provided answers.

These are adaptations of benchmark families cited by the CLM authors, not reproductions of their reported scores. We compare the released CLM head to its original Qwen3-8B backbone on the same Mac; Jev is not run here. A desired conclusion was not used to select test cases or suppress unfavorable outcomes.

Main finding: constrained Qwen selected the correct tool on 55/60 requests (91.7%), versus CLM’s 40/60 (66.7%), at approximately 81 versus 79 ms per cached decision. Qwen retained every CLM-correct answer and added 15. The paired accuracy difference is +25 percentage points, with a 95% bootstrap interval of +15 to +36.7 points.

The broader claim needs restraint: Wikipedia next-step scores were close to the random-link baseline, and the five actual races ended 1/5 for Qwen and 0/5 for CLM. Longer Qwen instructions changed BFCL from 79/90 to 78/90 and Wiki next-step accuracy from 15/40 to 16/40. The strongest supported result is a competitive constrained-decoding baseline with little cached latency overhead, not a general guarantee that longer prompts greatly improve accuracy.

Prompt engineering: what the experiment establishes

The short and longer Qwen prompts use identical weights and identical one-token constraints. Both were evaluated on the same held-out cases. A longer prompt is not automatically better; see the paired differences below. This experiment does not measure MLP training time or cost, so it cannot quantify how much easier prompt engineering is. No training is needed to deploy the Qwen baseline tested here.

Implementation and sampling

BF16 Qwen3-8B on Apple M4 Max, 64 GB, MLX; same loaded backbone for both paths. CLM uses the released approximately 18.9-million-parameter projection-head pair with normalized final hidden states; the port is checked against PyTorch. Qwen uses its original LM-head rows for verified single-token labels. It performs one state forward pass and argmax over only allowed labels, with no generation loop, thinking tokens, or additional completion pass. A–Z and verified single-token two-letter labels support larger menus. The full-vocabulary restricted-argmax audit is recorded below.

Development: 10 BFCL and 10 Wiki cases. Held-out: 90 BFCL and 40 Wiki cases. BFCL schemas are disjoint across development and test; Wiki targets are disjoint. Source-order deduplication and fixed random sampling happen before model scoring. Wiki source/target pairs come from human successful paths, have shortest distance 2–4, and retain every outgoing link. These are title-only observations, not full Wikipedia article reading. Most tool requests are short relative to their schemas; this suite does not replace the earlier RACE context-heavy experiment.
